1. Executive Summary
Study Clash is a competitive study platform for college and university students. Students create or join private groups with friends and classmates, record their study sessions, and compare their activity through group leaderboards, statistics, and a shared activity feed. The product turns studying into a friendly competition by making group members' study activity visible to one another.
The central idea is simple: motivate students to study more by appealing to their natural desire to compete, improve, and outperform their friends. Study Clash is intended to feel intense, social, and game-inspired rather than like a traditional private productivity tracker.

2. Problem Statement & Product Vision
Problem
Many students struggle to find the motivation to study consistently. Studying can feel repetitive, isolating, or easy to postpone, especially when there is no immediate sense of progress or accountability.
Proposed solution
Study Clash makes studying more engaging by allowing students to compete with friends in private groups. Members can see one another's study activity, compare statistics, participate in weekly and all-time rankings, and earn temporary recognition for winning or finishing last.
Core value proposition
·       Encourages students to study more.
·       Makes studying more enjoyable through competition.
·       Builds a competitive student community.
·       Makes study activity visible to a student's group instead of keeping progress entirely private.
Product personality
Competitive, intense, social, and gaming-inspired. The competitive tone should remain playful enough that the app encourages participation rather than discouraging students who are behind.
3. Target Audience & Groups                         	
Target audience
College and university students, particularly friends or classmates who want to motivate one another through friendly competition.
Group creation and membership
·       A student can create a group and invite friends.
·       Groups are intended to bring together students in the same program or cohort.
·       Groups are private and can be joined through an invite link or code.
·       A student can belong to multiple groups. (Skippable for hackathon scope)
·       There is no strict group-size limit and no minimum group size.
·       All members have equal permissions for ordinary group participation; the original creator is the exception for group deletion.
·       Members can leave a group whenever they want.
·       Only the original creator can delete the group.
 
Group experience
A group should feel like a small community or server, similar in concept to a Discord server. Members enter a group and navigate between distinct areas such as its leaderboard, chatroom, and activity feed.
4. Accounts & Student Profiles
Account creation
·       Users register using an email address and password.
·       No special student or school verification is required.
·       Users use their real first and last names as their username/display name.
·       Users select a profile picture from preset avatars.
·       Profiles do not display school-related information.
Profile visibility
A student's profile information and study statistics are visible to members of groups they share. Study activity and statistics should not be publicly visible outside those groups.
Profile statistics
·       Total study hours
·       Current group ranking
·       Study streak
·       Daily and weekly study hours
·       Longest study session
·       Recent study activity
A student may belong to multiple groups, so rankings should be understood in the context of a particular group rather than as one universal global rank.
Account deletion
Users can delete their own accounts. The exact deletion behaviour for associated study records, messages, and group ownership remains to be specified.

6. Study Tracking System
Ways to record study
·       The product vision includes both an in-app study timer and manual study-session entries.
·       Students can manually add a session they forgot to record.
·       Students can pause and resume an active timer.
·       A study session must be at least five minutes long.
·       Students can select a course or subject for each session.
·       Only one active study session is allowed per student at a time.
·       Students can view their own detailed study history.
·       Students can edit or delete their own study sessions.
Timer behaviour
·       When a student closes the app or browser, the active timer automatically pauses.
·       If a student simply forgets to stop a timer while the app remains open, it continues running until they stop it.
·       The interface should make the active, paused, and completed states clear.
IMPLEMENTATION DETAIL TO RESOLVE  The app needs a reliable way to detect or represent a closed session and restore its paused state when the user returns. A browser closing is not the same as a user pressing Pause.

 
Group visibility
Group members can see live study activity, completed sessions, and accumulated study hours. The activity feed should communicate when members start or finish studying.
Manual entries
Manual entries are allowed and are not visibly labelled differently from timer-recorded sessions. This is a confirmed product choice, although it means the system relies on honest reporting.
Minimum session and streak rules
A session must be at least five minutes. A student's streak increases for each day on which they study for at least 30 minutes.

8. Competition, Points & Rankings
Leaderboards
·       Each group has its own leaderboard.
·       There are weekly and all-time leaderboards.
·       Weekly rankings reset every Sunday at midnight; all-time totals remain.
·       Tied students share the same rank.
·       The leaderboard should combine ranks, points, gaps between players, and progress bars.
·       Member comparisons can include a mixture of study statistics.
Ranking formula
Rankings are based mostly on study time, with a smaller bonus for the number of study sessions. The exact weighting, point values, and any caps or minimum conditions have not yet been defined.
RECOMMENDATION  Use study minutes as the dominant score and add a modest session-completion bonus. Consider a minimum session duration (already set at five minutes) and a reasonable cap or diminishing return for session bonuses so that splitting one study block into many tiny sessions is not overly rewarded.

 
Streaks, challenges, and rewards
·       A streak day requires at least 30 minutes of study.
·       The app automatically creates group challenges.
·       There are no personal or group goals; the focus is competition.
·       Badges are awarded only for winning competitions.
·       Digital rewards may include trophies and titles.
·       The weekly winner receives a temporary crown displayed above their profile in the group chatroom.
·       The weekly loser (the member in last place) receives a temporary dunce hat displayed above their profile in the group chatroom.
·       Weekly winner/loser results are announced, as well as represented by profile decorations.
UNDECIDED  The exact challenge definitions, reward duration, and how to handle a one-member group or a tie for last place have not been specified.

 
Integrity approach
The product relies on honesty. There is no reporting system for suspicious study activity. This is a deliberate simplicity choice for the current plan, not a guarantee that all submitted study time is accurate.
9. Social Features & Group Interaction
Group chatroom
·       Every group has its own chatroom.
·       Chat supports text messages only.
·       Members can delete their own messages.
·       Chat and the study activity feed are separate sections.
Activity feed
The activity feed displays study events from group members, including study starts and finishes. It is intended to make group activity visible and create a sense of live competition.
Presence and notifications
·       The app shows whether members are online and whether they are currently studying.
·       Planned notification events: someone starts or finishes studying; weekly rankings change; the weekly winner is announced; a group member sends a chat message; a study streak is about to expire.
UNDECIDED  The delivery method for notifications has not been selected. In-app, browser, and email notifications remain possible implementation choices.

 
Moderation
No formal moderation features are planned. Members have equal permissions for normal group participation, but only the original creator can delete the group. Chat message deletion is available to each message's author.

11. Security, Privacy & Edge Cases
Privacy rules
·       Only members of a student's groups can see that student's study activity and statistics.
·       Group chat and activity data should only be readable by members of the relevant group.
·       Users can delete their own accounts.
·       No special student verification is required.
Access control
·       Users should only edit or delete their own study sessions.
·       Users should only delete their own chat messages.
·       Only the original creator can delete a group.
·       Group membership must be checked before allowing access to group content.
SECURITY RECOMMENDATION  Do not rely on hiding buttons in the frontend. Enforce group membership, creator-only group deletion, and user-owned edits/deletions in Firebase Security Rules and, where appropriate, trusted backend logic.

Security and privacy implementation checklist

·       Check group membership before returning group chat, activity, sessions, or statistics.

·       Enforce ownership checks for editing/deleting sessions and messages.
·       Do not expose invite codes or private group content through public profile pages.
·       Validate session durations and timestamps on the trusted side where practical.
·       Define what account deletion does to user-created groups and historical activity.

Recommendations for the team
·       Keep the hackathon MVP focused on the core competition loop rather than trying to finish every long-term feature.
·       Use Firebase Authentication, Firestore, and Firebase Hosting as the simplest likely fit for the selected stack.
·       Make group privacy and authorization rules part of the first implementation, not a last-minute addition.
·       Define a simple, transparent ranking formula before implementing the leaderboard. Avoid rewarding session count too heavily.
·       Treat weekly reset and reward assignment as one consistent weekly-results operation so rankings and decorations do not disagree.
·       Use a responsive web app for the hackathon rather than attempting separate native mobile applications.
·       Build the group interface around the Discord-like server concept: a clear group identity with distinct tabs for rankings, chat, and activity.
·       Document any shortcuts taken during the hackathon so they can be revisited after the event.
Final product statement
Study Clash is a private, group-based study competition platform that helps college and university students stay motivated by making their study activity visible to friends. Through group leaderboards, live activity, chat, streaks, and weekly rewards, it turns studying into a shared competition.
